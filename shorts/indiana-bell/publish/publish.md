## Description (YouTube / Facebook / Instagram)
In 1930, Indiana Bell needed a bigger headquarters right where its old eight-story telephone building stood. But that building ran the city's phone lines. Tear it down and the calls go silent.

So architect Kurt Vonnegut Sr. had a crazy idea: don't tear it down — move it.

Workers slid steel rollers under all 11,000 tons. Eighteen men worked the jacks, inching it along about 15 inches an hour: 52 feet south, a full 90-degree turn, then 100 feet west. About 600 employees kept working inside the whole time. Gas, water, power and phone lines stayed connected through flexible pipes and cables, people walked in over a moving bridge, and phone service never stopped.

And the architect's son? He grew up to be the novelist Kurt Vonnegut.
