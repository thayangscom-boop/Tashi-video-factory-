# Short #4 — "Why 21 Elephants Walked Across the Brooklyn Bridge" (draft, ~60 s)

Record it as one take, calm and a bit playful. Pauses at the "—" marks.

## Voiceover script (~150 words, about 60 s)
1. In May 1884, New York watched twenty-one elephants walk across the Brooklyn Bridge. Not for a parade. For a safety test.
2. The bridge had opened a year earlier, and it was the longest suspension bridge in the world. But people didn't trust it.
3. Then, one week after opening day, a rumor spread through the crowd: the bridge is collapsing. — A woman screamed on the stairs. — People panicked and ran. And about a dozen of them were crushed to death. The bridge never moved an inch.
4. Fear stuck anyway. People wouldn't cross.
5. So showman P. T. Barnum made an offer: let my circus walk across it.
6. At first, the city said no. Then they said yes.
7. On the evening of May seventeenth, thousands lined the riverbank. Barnum's elephants, led by their handlers, walked onto the bridge, one after another, with camels behind them.
8. The bridge held. Every step. — And New Yorkers started crossing again.
9. A circus stunt. A very big, very heavy proof. And the bridge is still standing today.
